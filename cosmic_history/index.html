Why the numbers differ

A stellar age is inferred by comparing measured properties with stellar-evolution models. Chemical abundances, opacity tables, mass, distance, temperature and oscillation frequencies affect the result. Different model assumptions can shift the estimated age substantially.

Why is it nearby?

Stars move. HD 140283 is a high-velocity halo star; its orbit and chemistry are consistent with an origin outside the Milky Way, plausibly associated with the Gaia-Enceladus merger. It could have been accreted into the Milky Way long after it formed.

What the 2025 asteroseismic study actually did

The team analysed 20-second-cadence TESS observations from Sector 51, identified solar-like oscillations including mixed modes, and combined them with literature constraints in the BASTA modelling framework. They reported 14.2 ± 0.4 Gyr (internal uncertainties), compatible with the Universe age within 1σ. The authors caution that systematic effects are not fully included and discuss potentially larger realistic uncertainties.

Evidence register

Scientific limits

• This is a literature-comparison prototype, not a new independent age measurement.

• The plotted values come from published studies and a workshop poster; preliminary results are labelled as such.

• Do not average the estimates: they use different input data and model assumptions, and are not independent measurements.

• The 2025 value 14.2 ± 0.4 Gyr quotes internal modelling uncertainty only. It does not mean the star is older than the Universe.
